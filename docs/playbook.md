## Tracks

1. Longevity biology and AI. Three written challenges, each with a required output and a suggested open-data stack: find the process limiting restoration of a younger state (ranked hypotheses, cross-tissue evidence, stated uncertainty, one falsifying experiment); go from an ageing phenotype to a target and a chemical perturbation (predicted direction, independent evidence layers, controls, a validation experiment); choose the next experiment with the highest information gain from conflicting evidence (competing hypotheses, predicted outcomes, a decision rule). Pre-curated IDs and feature matrices are provided. One tissue, perturbation, compound class, or evidence layer is held out for evaluation. Uncertainty and a falsification criterion are required. Biological validity scores above UI polish.
2. AI infrastructure on Nebius. The brief, access route, and documentation arrive before the event.
3. Communication, trust, and policy. Four written challenges: a cited, time-stamped map of political engagement with longevity, including an explicit "nothing reliable found" state; a classifier for how much public research funding targets ageing biology (Swecris, CORDIS, NIH RePORTER); an evidence-chain audit of a longevity claim (PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, with an evidence ladder from cells to trials); a multi-agent design of a EUR 10 billion ageing research portfolio with a red-team pass and a short white paper. Every finding links to a dated source, uncertainty is part of the output, and the system is checked against a small human-reviewed sample before scaling.
4. Healthspan and clinical translation. Clinician-facing tools and preventive-health workflows. No written brief.
5. Open wildcard. Propose your own challenge.

Every brief says the same thing about scope: a narrow, well-checked result beats a broad but unreliable one.

## Submission

- Deadline Sunday 12:00. One team member asks the organisers for access to upload the repo to the [hackathon repository](https://github.com/OxbridgeFrontier/Stockholm-AI-x-Longevity-Hack-2026).
- Demo Day components to prepare: problem and intended user; what was built and why; architecture and tools; data sources, licences, and evidence; working demo plus a recorded fallback; results and success metrics; limitations, risks, and safety; team and next steps.
- The suggested workflow is define the problem and success metric, choose and document data, build the smallest testable prototype, validate honestly including failure modes, then prepare the demo.
- Datasets outside the library are allowed if the team has the right to use them. Document source and licence. Restricted, paid, private, or identifiable data needs explicit permission and safeguards.
- Teams keep full ownership of what they build.

Track emphasis: track 1 scientific validity and evaluation; track 2 performance, reliability, and scalability; track 3 trust, audience fit, adoption, and distribution; track 4 clinical relevance, workflow fit, and safety.

## Conduct

Ask before photographing or recording people. Never use identifiable patient or confidential personal data. Be explicit about what the prototype can and cannot do, especially anything medical. Respect other people's work, licences, and attribution.
