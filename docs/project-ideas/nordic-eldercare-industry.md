## Assessment

The main contradiction in elderly care in the Nordic region is that service demand is growing faster than the supply of qualified labor. Technology has entered policy and municipal practice, but industry bottlenecks usually appear after deployment: workflows do not change in sync, systems struggle to collaborate, actual usage rates are low, effectiveness is not uniform, and elderly people or staff cannot use them smoothly.

This concentrates industry opportunities in three categories: reducing the information and coordination burden on caregivers; Helping organizations determine whether a technology truly improves outcomes; Deploy limited manpower without sacrificing continuity or safety. Directly making diagnoses or risk decisions for elderly patients requires significantly higher data, clinical validation, and supervision.

## Why demand continues to grow

By early 2025, the five Nordic countries will have a combined population of about 28.3 million. Compared to 2000, the population aged 65 and over increased by 31%, while those aged 20 to 64 decreased by 3%.[Nordic Statistics,2025-05-02](https://www.nordicstatistics.org/news/the-nordic-population-2025/) Another set of official Nordic forecasts shows that the proportion of people aged 65 and above will rise from 20% in 2022 to 25% by 2040.[Nordic Council of Ministers, accessed 2026-09-08](https://www.norden.org/en/information/population-nordic-region)

The pressure is uneven. Nordregio predicts that by 2045, almost all Nordic municipalities will face an aging population increase; Many peripheral cities and suburban towns may see population over 80 years old grow by 75% or more. In 2023, Finland transferred the responsibility for elderly care to Wellbeing Services Counties, while most other Nordic countries still mainly bear the burden of municipalities.[State of the Nordic Region 2026,2026](https://pub.nordregio.org/r-2026-2-state-of-the-nordic-region-2026/population-change-in-the-nordic-region.html)

The OECD expects long-term care demand to grow by more than one-third of member countries by 2050. The Nordic formal care system covers a wide range and is accompanied by high public spending: in 2021, Norway, Sweden, and Denmark spent about 3.5%, 3.4%, and 3.2% of GDP on long-term care. The caliber of cross-country statistics differs; these figures are suitable for explaining fiscal pressure and cannot directly compare the efficiency of a single municipal service.[OECD,2023](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/health-at-a-glance-2023_7a7afb35-en/full-report/long-term-care-spending-and-unit-costs_cc989a20.html)

## Five Countries Focus

### Denmark

Denmark's new elderly care law will take effect on July 1, 2025. The reform centers on elderly autonomy, trust in staff and frontline management, and collaboration with families, communities, and NGOs; Service design emphasizes continuous, holistic, and cross-disciplinary care.[Danish Health Authority,2024-12-05](https://www.sst.dk/nyheder/2024/Nyt-partnerskab-skal-styrke-aeldreplejen) This addresses issues of fragmented services and insufficient autonomy among frontline personnel.

Technology policy has shifted from pilot to expansion. The implementation community of the Danish Local Government Association KL prioritizes support for screen access and digital-assisted training, and requires measurement of the value and effectiveness of the technology. The agency also explicitly lists workflow transformation and regulatory requirements as part of the AI and welfare technology expansion.[KL, updated on 2025-10-02](https://www.kl.dk/sundhed-og-aeldre/aeldrereformen/implementering-af-aeldrereformen/velfaerdsteknologi-i-aeldreplejen) The OECD's 2024 Danish Economic Survey predicts that if the caregiver-to-elderly ratio is maintained, the number of care workers needed will increase by about 50% by 2040 compared to then; This is a model prediction influenced by changes in population, productivity, and policy.[OECD,2024](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-denmark-2024_d5c6f307-en/full-report/component-5.html)

### Finland

In 2023, Finland transferred health and social service responsibilities to Wellbeing Services Counties. THL pointed out that both home care and all-day accommodation services face complex needs; According to 2022 data, about half of home-based care clients experienced cognitive decline, and around 70% of all-day accommodation clients had a dementia diagnosis.[THL,2024-03-27](https://blogi.thl.fi/tiedolla-johtaminen-on-taitolaji-myos-iakkaiden-palveluja-suunniteltaessa/) This means simply expanding remote services cannot cover everyone; service tiering and 24-day care capacity still need to be retained.

Finland already has an RAI comparison database and monthly updated home care access data, supporting regional and provider comparisons, but units with fewer than 30 assessment targets are vulnerable to random fluctuations.[THL RAI database, accessed 2026-09-08](https://sampo.thl.fi/pivot/prod/sv/rai/first/summary_t3viivakuvio) Nordic Welfare Centre's 2023 stockshoot records: THL launched the KATI project in 2020, supporting smart aging and home care technologies. The OECD 2026 report estimates that Finland will have only about 4 long-term care workers per 100 people aged 65 and over, which is lower than the OECD average at the time and Sweden and Norway; Different data sources do not fully agree on the occupational classification of "long-term care workers."[OECD,2026](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2026/04/well-being-along-the-life-course-in-finland_435c6448/4bddb2df-en.pdf)

### Iceland

Iceland's current population structure is younger than most Nordic countries, but Nordic Statistics' existing forecasts suggest that from 2022 to 2040, the population aged 65 and over could increase by 52%, the highest among the five countries.[Nordic Statistics, accessed 2026-09-08](https://www.nordicstatistics.org/news/population-forecasts-the-demographic-future-of-the-nordic-region/) The OECD 2025 report lists 9 long-term care workers per 100 people aged 65 and over, higher than the OECD average of 5.[OECD,2025](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/health-at-a-glance-2025_15a55280-en/iceland_c86b5ca6-en.html)

The Nordic Welfare Centre's 2023 inventory stated that Iceland did not have national dedicated welfare tech investments of the same scale as the other four countries, but Reykjavík and other regions already had local projects. This suggests that project maturity and governance may be more region-dependent. Existing searches lack sufficient first-hand data on recent elderly care wait times, staff mobility, and actual technology use in Iceland, making it inappropriate to draw more detailed national conclusions.[Nordic Welfare Centre,2023-08-23](https://nordicwelfare.org/en/publikationer/valfardsteknologi-i-de-nordiska-landerna-en-kartlaggning-av-statliga-satsningar/)

### Norway

Norway's "Safe Home" reform enables more seniors to stay safely at home through housing planning, prevention, targeted services, fixed teams, staff capacity, and family support.[Norwegian Government, 2023-2024 Budget Notes](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/prop.-1-s-20232024/id2997928/?ch=1) The government also acknowledges that home living is not suitable for everyone, and the 2026 budget continues to plan for full-day care beds. Therefore, "home-first" should be understood as service tiering, not closing institutional care.

The Health Personnel Plan 2040, released in 2026, cites Statistics Norway and predicts: Based on current trends, the shortage of health and care service staff may increase from about 9,900 full-time equivalents in 2025 to about 42,400 in 2040, with pressure concentrated in nursing, care assistants, institutional, and home services. This forecast is not a definitive outcome, but it is sufficient to show that staffing, retention, and productivity are policy priorities.[Norwegian Government, 2026](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/meld.-st.-11-20252026/id3164917/?ch=1)

### Sweden

Sweden is leaving more care within the home environment, but those receiving services often have greater needs. By 2025, the average number of home care workers responsible for services will rise from 9.16 on weekdays to 10.06 on weekdays, and from 11.67 on weekends to 12.58 on weekends; 74% of home care recipients are provided with a fixed care contact.[Socialstyrelsen,2025-10-09](https://www.socialstyrelsen.se/statistik-och-data/oppna-jamforelser/socialtjanst/aldreomsorg/hemtjanst-och-sarskilt-boende/) In 2024, people who receive at least two in-home services daily will have an average of 16 contacts over 14 days, indicating that fixed contacts have not resolved personnel continuity issues.[Socialstyrelsen,2025](https://www.socialstyrelsen.se/contentassets/81c2165f140b481ca31c21abfe9dec46/2025-3-9487.pdf)

There is a gap between the coverage and use of welfare technology. The Socialstyrelsen 2025 survey shows that the number of reported users for digital monitoring, GPS alerts, and automated dispensing is increasing, but some municipalities that already provide technology to target populations still have no users or only a small number of users.[Socialstyrelsen,2025](https://www.socialstyrelsen.se/globalassets/sharepoint-dokument/artikelkatalog/ovrigt/2025-5-9625.pdf) Therefore, Sweden is well suited to study "why deployment hasn't translated into use" and "how to quantify the value of technology," rather than just developing a prototype again.

## Which Solutions Have the Strongest Practical Foundation?

Home support combined with rehabilitation, fixed contacts or small teams, remote access, digitally assisted training, automated dispensing, and safety alerts have entered policies or municipal practices in some countries. Their common conditions are that seniors still have suitable functional status, staff receive training, technology is integrated into existing processes, and manual processing and non-digital alternative channels are retained.

AI is better suited to enter low-risk, manually re-verified stages first, such as record drafting, handover verification, cross-record change summarization, scheduling constraint checks, and evidence audits. Any risk prediction system needs to clearly define the prediction time window, target audience, result labels, costs for false and missed reports, and the responsible person after the alarm. Cameras, sensors, and voice data also handle informed consent, family member privacy, retention periods, and access audits.

"Successful pilots" are not enough to support scaling. In 2017, the Nordic Welfare Centre documented the difficulties faced by more than 1,200 Nordic municipalities in transitioning welfare technology from projects into everyday services; Denmark's current approach is to focus on supporting a few more mature technologies and providing common tools for implementation and effectiveness measurement.[Nordic Welfare Centre,2017-03-10](https://nordicwelfare.org/en/publikationer/welfare-technology-tool-box/);[KL, updated on 2025-10-02](https://www.kl.dk/sundhed-og-aeldre/aeldrereformen/implementering-af-aeldrereformen/velfaerdsteknologi-i-aeldreplejen)

### N4. Two-person synchronous scheduling experiment (started after data review)

Public data for Strathclyde (9) comes from a care provider in the UK, covering 2017-10-01 to 2017-10-14, licensed under ODC-BY. carers.csv Provide staff dates and available times; visits.csv Provide visit dates, preferred times, durations, and the number of people needed; distance.csv Provide travel times between visit locations. Fields support shift and two-person synchronization constraints, but cannot supplement qualifications, familiar personnel, or patient preferences.[Data explanation from the publisher, 2018-10-25; list verification on 2026-09-09](https://pureportal.strath.ac.uk/en/datasets/dataset-of-home-care-scheduling-and-routing-problems-with-synchro/)

Before starting, check the README, public authorization, de-identification instructions, and file scope according to the checklist; If restricted, private, or identifiable data is involved, it shall be handled according to[Warehouse rules](../../AGENTS.md) Obtain clear authority and implement protective measures. No scheduling records were downloaded this round, nor was it determined that any personally identifiable information existed. The review is not yet complete, the project remains conditional candidate.

After passing, simple greedy arrangements are used as the baseline and compared with constrained optimizers. The acceptable time window around the preferred time is fixed according to the README or pre-declared experimental hypotheses; Infeasible instances must display conflicts and cannot be improved by deleting difficult access. Report unassigned visits, time window violations, two-person synchronization failures, travel time, and solving time; compare feasibility first, then compare itineraries.
